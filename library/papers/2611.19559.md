# ResWAM: Learning What Changes, Not What Stays, for World-Action Models

> 机器人策略学习里有一个长期被忽视的问题：现有的世界-动作模型在训练时，要求模型预测未来的完整视觉观测，但机器人在执行一段动作的过程中，场景里绝大多数东西根本就没动。桌面、墙壁、灯光、背景——这些静止的内容在前后两帧之间几乎一模一样。论文作者

**摘要 (EN):** World-action models (WAMs) augment vision-language-action policies (VLAs) with future prediction so that the policy can anticipate how its actions will affect the physical world. Existing WAMs supervise complete future observations. However, observations typically change little over an action chunk, so the future loss is dominated by copying static background content rather than learning task-relevant robot and object motion, limiting policy learning. Moreover, existing WAMs rely on large video generation models or separate world models, incurring heavy training and inference compute that hinders closed-loop deployment. To improve policy performance, we propose visual residual learning, supervising only the feature change from the current frame to the future frame in a frozen DINOv3 space. Compared with complete future features, this residual learning target yields a higher feature rank with richer and more diverse representations of scene changes, and spatially concentrates on the robot arm and manipulated objects rather than the background. To further improve computational efficiency, we present ResWAM, a residual world-action model with 2.3B parameters in total, including a lightweight 0.5B dual-branch DiT that jointly trains an action branch and a residual branch via conditional flow matching. Without robot pretraining (i.e., pretraining on external robot action trajectories beyond the benchmark demonstrations), ResWAM achieves top average success rates on LIBERO-Plus (81.1%), RoboTwin 2.0 (93.8%), and RoboDojo (9.40% success, 13.41 score) at the lowest training (2.7-4.0 TFLOPs) and inference compute (1.0-1.4 TFLOPs), and leads real-robot Open, Put, and Stack (80.0% average, +10.0% over π_{0.5}) at 92.7 ms latency.
