# Breath-Dx: A Time Series Classification Benchmark for Disease Screening with eNose

> 呼出的气体里藏着疾病的信号——这个想法听起来像科幻，但它其实已经有几十年的研究积累。问题是，这个领域至今没有一个可靠的、公开的、能让不同研究组相互比较的基准。每篇论文用自己的小数据集、自己的评估方式，结果根本没法横向比较，更不用说找到真正能

**摘要 (EN):** Electronic nose (eNose) technology offers a portable, non-invasive route for disease screening via exhaled breath analysis. However, progress in the field is hindered by the lack of systematic benchmarks that compare machine learning methods under realistic conditions, particularly sensor drift, a known but rarely quantified failure mode. Most existing studies rely on small, private datasets with inconsistent protocols, rendering results non-comparable and slowing progress. We introduce Breath-Dx, the first comprehensive benchmark of time series classification methods for breath-based disease screening. Our work leverages the Scent of Health (S-OH) dataset, the largest public clinical eNose collection to date, comprising 1,234 patients across nine diagnostic groups, each providing a 17-channel multivariate time series with explicit temporal train/test splits that account for sensor drift. We systematically evaluate 10 methods spanning four families: feature-based tabular learners, convolution-based models, graph-based, and time series architectures. All methods degrade significantly under temporal validation (0.05-0.12 AUC drop compared to random cross-validation), confirming that drift embeds a strong temporal signal. Under this realistic evaluation, the best methods achieve ROC AUC of 0.74-0.76 for some lung conditions, though others remain near chance. Recurrent architectures show the most consistent performance across diseases, while specialized time series methods achieve the highest peak results. Demographic confounders matter: breath signals provide independent diagnostic value for some conditions but add little beyond age for others. We release the S-OH dataset, all temporal splits, evaluation protocols, and code to enable reproducible research and accelerate progress in clinical breathomics.
