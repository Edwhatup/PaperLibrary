# The Resolution Hypothesis: Agent Evolution's Key Is Identifying Improvements

> 智能体进化领域有一个反复出现的谜题：同一套方法，在这篇论文里有效，在另一篇论文里就失效了；某个方法在一个基准上大幅领先，换个基准就原地踏步。今天要聊的这篇论文，给出了一个相当有说服力的解释，他们把它称为"分辨率假说"。

**摘要 (EN):** Agent evolution follows a simple loop: a proposer writes candidate artifacts (prompts, skills, memories) and selection accepts those that score higher. This loop has shown promise, yet practitioners face a puzzle: methods trade wins across papers, and evolution often fails to bootstrap on certain tasks. We trace this puzzle to the Resolution Hypothesis: agent evolution stalls not because methods fail to propose better artifacts, but because noisy evaluations cannot reliably identify them. Across seven evolution methods and six models (over 10M LLM calls), we find support for the hypothesis at both levels where evaluation scores are used. At the candidate level (choosing artifacts), helpful candidate artifacts exist, but selection is no better than random at telling them from harmful ones, so errors accumulate across rounds. At the method level (ranking methods), typical margins of a few percentage points are smaller than the difference between two independent runs of the same method, so published rankings often rest on luck. Together, these findings indicate that agent evolution is often bottlenecked by reliably identifying improvements. This calls for a shift in how we develop and evaluate self-improving agents, from designing ever more candidate improvement systems to making true improvements detectable and accumulable.
