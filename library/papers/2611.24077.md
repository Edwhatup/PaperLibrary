# AeroRSI: Recursive Self-Improvement for Aerial Vision-Language Navigation

> 让无人机听懂自然语言指令、自主完成飞行任务，这个目标听起来很美，但现实中存在一个顽固的障碍：用人类示范数据训练出来的飞行策略，在真实闭环飞行中会频繁出错，撞上障碍物、偏离目标、提前降落。而且因为一次错误就能终结整段飞行，这些失败对任务完成率

**摘要 (EN):** Vision-language-action (VLA) models enable unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) to follow natural-language instructions, yet still fail frequently in closed-loop flight. Correcting these failures requires repairs, i.e., actions that avoid them at the states the policy itself reaches, which human pilots cannot label at scale and rule-based planners cannot supply reliably. In this paper, we present AeroRSI, a recursive self-improvement framework in which an aerial navigation policy iteratively learns from its own failures. Our key insight is that a failing policy should judge its own repairs by flying on from them. Judged in this way, its sampled actions and even rule-based proposals that hurt it when imitated directly become reliable supervision. Moreover, an episode terminates at its first failure, concealing subsequent failures until the earlier ones are repaired. AeroRSI thus renews its supervision in every round, anchoring the failures of the current policy, verifying repairs by its continuation, and distilling them while keeping its behavior elsewhere. On TravelUAV, three rounds raise the success rate of a supervised 2B-parameter policy from 46.4% to 67.5% and reduce collisions by half, surpassing the previous state of the art by 10.6 points from the same initialization without extra human labels or inference cost. Code and models are available at https://github.com/starsholic/AeroRSI.
